I want this services practice to behave like software.

A working thesis

Compound is that bet — AI doing more of the work, judgment staying human, every engagement making the next one sharper.

The work that compounds.

The idea

Sell the finished outcome. Keep the work that makes it possible.

Software leaves the customer to do the work. Traditional services sell people's time to do it. I'm interested in a practice that sits between those two models. AI handles much of the production. I handle the judgment, the context, and the decisions that still need a person. The work stays inside the practice and gets sharper with every engagement.

How the work gets done

AI does the production work

Research, analysis, drafting, data work, and other repeatable parts of the job.

I own the judgment

The context, the decisions, the exceptions, and the accountability for the result.

The workflow stays with me

Not handed over and forgotten. Every engagement should make it sharper than the last — that's the actual bet behind Compound.

A couple of early tests.

Two early conversations — before Compound had a name, and before either became a formal engagement.

Positioning a creative production studio

A creative production studio serving prestige beauty brands was preparing for a new go-to-market and building a new credentials deck. The challenge was that its story could have belonged to almost any studio in the category.

I rebuilt the positioning around one sharp claim: a brand that charges a premium can't afford execution that undersells it.

Reframing an AI sales story

An AI company building agents for financial-services workflows shared a sales deck they wanted to sharpen. I ran it through a structured process of research, analysis, and independent verification.

The real gap was that the deck spoke to the person who feels the pain, not the person who signs the contract.

Where I start

The work,
not the industry.

I'm deliberately starting with a kind of work, not a category of company.

I'm looking for work that costs a company real money today, happens over and over, and that AI can already do a meaningful share of — so the result is something I can actually point to and measure.

The question

Can the work itself become the advantage?

That's what I'm trying to find out. If every engagement makes the next one better, faster, and cheaper to serve, Compound starts to behave like a product company on the inside. If it doesn't, it's a services practice that uses AI well — nothing more.
