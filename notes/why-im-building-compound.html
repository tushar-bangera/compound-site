Why I'm building Compound

A working thesis, not a finished company.

I've spent fifteen years on the client-facing side of professional services — running enterprise accounts, sitting with founders and brand leaders, owning outcomes that showed up in my numbers when they went wrong.

That job taught me something the software industry is only now catching up to. Getting something built was never the hard part. Making it work inside one specific, messy, real organization — and staying accountable for the result — was always the harder problem.

AI has changed the economics of that job, not the job itself.

For years, the discipline of owning an outcome — not just handing over a deliverable — belonged almost entirely to services firms. Now AI can do a real share of the production work behind that discipline: the research, the drafting, the analysis. What's left for a person is what was always the hard part. Deciding what matters. Reading the client. Being on the hook when it doesn't work.

Compound is a bet on that discipline, rebuilt with AI doing the work and a person owning the result.

I don't have a market chosen yet. I've looked at companies already doing versions of this — in insurance, law, healthcare, fund administration — and none of them tell me what Compound should do. What they tell me is this isn't a theory. People are already paying for work delivered this way.

What I bring isn't deep expertise in any of those categories. It's fifteen years of knowing how services businesses actually earn trust and keep it. Whether that's enough is the question I'm testing now, not one I've already answered.

—Tushar
